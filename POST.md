My football simulator needed to shout GOOOOAAALLL.

Recording someone is the obvious answer, but then it's the same shout every goal forever and
everyone clocks it by the third one. The browser's built-in text-to-speech was worse — it reads
"GOOOAL" as *gull*.

So I decided to build a throat instead. From physics. How hard could it be.

Nine attempts in, it had sounded — in this order — like a ghost, a sasquatch, a cow, a Spanish
speaker, and a sheep.

Every one of those turned out to be a real diagnosis. The cow was a mouth that wasn't open
enough. The sheep was the loudness wobbling a few times a second, which is precisely what a bleat
is. The Spanish speaker was sounds not quite arriving where they were going — which is genuinely
one of the things an accent is.

Somewhere in there it stopped being about football.

It's now a vocal tract you can play with in a browser: 44 tubes, a tongue, and the arithmetic of
a wave hitting a change in cross-section. No recordings, no samples. It reads Shakespeare and
Moby-Dick. You can make it a giant, or a mouse, or Barry White.

It still can't say "j". *Jump* comes out as *doo-ump*.

But it can say *hello world* in something that sounds like a person, built out of nothing but a
tube and a description of how air behaves.

And when it shouts GOOOOAAALLL, it's a different shout every time. Which is what I wanted in the
first place.
